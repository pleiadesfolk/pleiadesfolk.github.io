Field psychological safety toolkit

This is a field psychological safety toolkit that's meant primarily to help you with keeping yourself safe.

There are three parts to this toolkit:

  • A self check-in tool
  • Making your own Pocket Crisis Plans:
    • Having a plan for when you're in a psychological crisis
    • Having a plan for when you're feeling unsafe and may hurt yourself
    • Having a plan for when you're on the brink of relapsing
  • An aftercare guide for when you've hurt yourself

The first part of this toolkit is a checklist to ensure that you have taken care of your own personal bodily needs. Whether you are involved in caring for your body, no matter how you feel about it, caring for it is part of system accountability. So too, is stopping yourself from self-harm, addiction, or suicidal behaviour.

It's important to note that there's a major difference between feeling like you want to do something and actually doing it, and this is what the crisis plans are about — stopping you from engaging in these activities, and making sure that you don't fall back into these behaviours.

You should make these plans when you're okay, so that it's ready when you need it.

Note

The Field Psychological Safety Toolkit is based on four different elements: You Feel Like Shit, LB Lee's Pocket Crisis Guide, Samaritans' Editable Safety Plan (among others), as well as a standard Addiction Relapse Prevention Plan.

These are not surefire ways to keep you from, say, hurting yourself. But they are tools designed to make you aware of what a crisis looks like and what may trigger you.

At the end of each form, you'll be able to export a typed-up version of your plan as a printable A4 zine — we don't store anything on a server, so save it when it's generated.

This is your regular reminder that the Pleiadesfolk and this website's authors are not professionals. We are merely members of a community that make tools that help people! While these plans are based on existing self-care toolkits and adapted for a more plural context, they have not been tested at scale.

This toolkit is also primarily written from a harm reduction lens. We're not keen on judging people — and that's why we also have an after-crisis plan, for when you've done a Bad Thing and you need to deal with it.
