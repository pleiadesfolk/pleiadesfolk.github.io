Emergency relapse prevention plan

This form helps you build an emergency relapse prevention plan on paper, and is an adaptation of some standard relapse prevention plans like this one.

While it’s possible to save this plan into your Notes app of choice, having a hard copy version of this will help you in case you need to refer to this — it gives your plan some physicality to it.

At the end of this form, you’ll be able to export a typed-up version of your pocket crisis plan, save it, and print it out! This will however be an A4-sized pdf file (sorry Americans!).

We don’t store the PDFs on a server, so you’ll have to save it to your computer or phone when it’s generated.

If you aren't able to export the PDFs, or simply want the zine template, you can find it here.

a) What are some high risk situations for relapse?

List out the people, places, and things you associate with your addiction, as well as situations where you may be very stressed.

When are you more prone to substance use? Are some people triggers? Why did you relapse before, and what happened then?

This is not a judgement of past you — it's an objective inventory of what's gone wrong before, so you don't make the same mistakes again.

b) What are some "red flag" thought patterns?

What are the thoughts you might have had that led to relapse? Were you browsing certain content? Do anniversaries or certain times of year trigger things? What feelings are linked to them?

Like before, this is an objective inventory — to help you not make the same mistakes again.

c) What are some attitude or behavioural changes that I've noticed when I relapsed in the past?

This is where you look at changes in your own attitudes, beliefs, and behaviours that may be a sign that could lead to relapse.

What are some of the behavioural changes that you've noticed? What are some of the changes to your attitudes that you've seen?

d) Who can I talk to if I am on the verge of relapse?

When you're facing a relapse, it can be helpful to lean on the people around you. How can they help you? What can they do for you?

Have at least three people you can reach out to. Talk to them before you put them in your plan — just to prepare them for what they might expect and what you might need.

Having an effective circle of support is an important aspect of recovery — don't keep yourself isolated! Have support from family — found family or blood relatives — as well as friends.

e) What are some of the coping techniques I can adopt?

List what you can do instead of relapsing. It could be anything, really — getting a massage, exercising, meditating, reading, journalling, repeating affirmations, talking with people who love you.

It's essential that your coping techniques manage warning signs effectively — and that they're healthy.

f) How can I make sure I'm on the right track?

List a few ways you can keep yourself committed to this plan. Who can keep you accountable? How can you keep yourself accountable?

It's also important that your plans are updated and revisited (hopefully with a professional) as you change and grow and your needs evolve.

How to fold

Your answers are never sent to a server. The PDF is generated in your browser and opens in a new tab — save it from there.
